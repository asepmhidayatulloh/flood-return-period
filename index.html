How it works and limitations

The valley terrain is generated synthetically (meandering channel, floodplain, natural levee, a low-lying depression and valley walls). For each return period:

  • Peak discharge comes from the Gumbel quantile, QT = mean × (1 + KT × Cv), or from your manual input.
  • At every cross-section the water surface elevation is found from Manning's equation with strip-wise conveyance, so channel and floodplain use different n.
  • The water surface is mapped across the grid. Only cells connected to the channel count as flooded.
  • Local velocity is v = h2/3 √S / n, directed along the valley axis. Arrows and animated tracer particles show this field. Animation speed is a visual time-lapse, not real time.

This is an educational steady-flow model, not a replacement for HEC-RAS. It has no flood-wave routing, backwater, bridges or true 2D flow. For real studies use a DEM, surveyed geometry and calibration.
